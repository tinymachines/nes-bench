# The cart reader: V15.6 with the serial updater, and the SD card

**Firmware: done.** The OSCR runs V15.6 and answers on serial.
**SD card: open, and it is yours.** The card is inside the reader and
nothing here can reach it; the files are staged and waiting.

## Why it was silent

The reader appears on the Pi as a CH340 (`/dev/ttyUSB0`). It said
nothing at any baud rate from 1200 to 2M, prompted or not, with the
handshake lines in every state.

That was not a fault. Three measurements found it:

1. **The Mega 2560's bootloader answered.** A stk500v2 `CMD_SIGN_ON` at
   115200 right after a DTR reset returned `AVRISP_2`, so the board was
   powered and the CH340 to Serial0 path worked in both directions.
2. **Its flash held `OSCR HW5`** and the per-console strings, so the
   firmware was the right family.
3. **Its flash did not hold `VERCHK`, `GETCLOCK` or `ETVOLTS`.** Those
   are plain string literals and would sit in the image if the code had
   them. The firmware simply had no serial command interface, which
   matches V12.1: the interface came later.

Everything above was read-only. Only SIGN_ON, ENTER and LEAVE PROGMODE,
LOAD_ADDRESS and READ_FLASH were ever constructed. CHIP_ERASE and
PROGRAM_FLASH were not built at all.

## What was done

Built from the tree already cloned at `6502/extern/cartreader`, master
at `060d8ae`, which is `V15.6`. Firmware and databases therefore come
from **one commit** and are paired by construction.

- `Config.h` with `#define HW5` and nothing else changed. HW5 brings in
  the LCD, the rotary encoder and the NeoPixel, and keeps
  `ENABLE_UPDATER`, which is what this was for. **`SERIAL_MONITOR` was
  not used**: that one replaces the display, and the point here was to
  add a serial channel without giving up the screen.
- **181,860 bytes, 71% of flash. 5,234 bytes of RAM, 63%.** Room left.
- The old firmware was read off the part first and kept.
- Written and verified by avrdude 8.0 running **on the Pi**, against
  `/dev/ttyUSB0` directly. avrdude is on neither machine and the Pi
  cannot install anything, so the aarch64 build was fetched here and
  copied over.

## What the reader answers now

At **9600 baud**, on boot and on demand:

```
OSCR::V15.6//HW5|CLOCKGEN|FASTCRC
```

| command | answer |
|---|---|
| `VERCHK` | the version and feature string above |
| `GETCLOCK` | `Module is not enabled.` (`ENABLE_3V3FIX` is off) |
| `GETVOLTS`, `SETVOLTS 3\|5` | `Module is not enabled.` (no VSELECT module) |
| `GETTIME`, `SETTIME <epoch>` | needs the RTC module, which is off |

The two "not enabled" answers are correct rather than a fault: they name
modules this hardware does not have. The screen and the dial work as
before.

To reach it from the workstation, the same bridge the Uno uses, on
another port:

```
python3 ~/serial-bridge.py --port /dev/ttyUSB0 --baud 9600 --listen 0.0.0.0:6546
```

## The SD card, which is yours to do

The card is in the reader and nothing here can write it. The V15.6
databases are staged in two places:

- on the Pi: `~/oscr-sd-V15.6/`, 45 files, 2.2 MB
- on the workstation: `~/oscr-backup/oscr-sd-V15.6.tar.gz`, and the
  originals at `6502/extern/cartreader/sd/`

**Steps:**

1. Switch the reader off and take the card out.
2. Put it in a PC. **Do not format it and do not delete anything**: the
   numbered folders are your dumps.
3. Copy all 45 files into the **root** of the card, overwriting.
4. Card back in, reader on.

**What the refresh buys, measured against the V12.1 files:**

| database | V12.1 | V15.6 |
|---|---|---|
| `nes.txt` | 9,330 lines | **22,143 lines** |
| `gba.txt` | 10,071 | 11,055 |
| `md.txt` | 8,034 | 8,094 |
| `n64.txt` | 3,072 | 3,087 |
| `snes.txt` | 11,714 | 11,717 |

Plus 25 database files that did not exist in V12.1. **The NES database
more than doubled**, which is the one that matters here.

`config.txt` is in the set and is inert: `ENABLE_CONFIG` is off in this
build, so nothing reads it.

## Rolling back

The firmware that was on it is kept at
`~/oscr-backup/oscr-before-V15.6-flash-2026-09-10.hex` on the
workstation and at `/tmp/oscr-backup-before.hex` on the Pi, which the Pi
loses on reboot. 8,173 Intel HEX records, ending in a proper EOF record.
The new image is beside it as `oscr-V15.6-HW5-updater.hex`.
